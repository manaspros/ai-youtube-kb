> GR's Ross and Chengxi Taylor trace RL-for-reasoning from Galactica and 2022 PPO recipes to long-horizon agents: value models, compaction, pipeline RL, and a benchmark where frontier models lost money.

## Summary
Ross Taylor, CEO of London RL company General Reasoning (GR) and former reasoning lead at Meta, recounts the 2022 era: Galactica, a base model shipped as a demo without an RL pipeline, versus ChatGPT, which had RLHF and became the product. He describes his own early attempt to run PPO with verifiable rewards on Llama 2 math training, which gave strong math results but no inference-time reflection, and attributes that to weak base models. Co-founder Chengxi Taylor argues long-horizon work is a mindset as well as an engineering problem, starting from a context window far smaller than the reasoning a hard task needs. Their proposed approach is to train compaction (summarize when the window fills) jointly with the task via RL, backed by a value model. Long-horizon RL has three problems: gradient variance grows with trajectory length, rewards are sparse, and trajectory lengths vary. On KellyBench, a one-year task where agents build models to trade Premier League matches starting from $100K, every frontier model lost money, which the speakers attribute to coding-heavy training, closed-ended benchmarks and missing multi-agent simulation. On compute, pipeline RL overlaps training with rollout generation but bounds off-policy lag (about 8 steps in their experience), and value-based bootstrapping helps when rollouts take weeks. The talk ends with a brief plug for OpenReward, a hosted environment platform with 350+ environments behind one API.

## Key ideas
- **A good base model is not enough; RLHF is what made Galactica-era LLMs into products** — The speaker contrasts Galactica (base model shipped as a demo, quirky and hallucinating) with ChatGPT (same class of base model plus an RLHF pipeline). He uses this as a natural experiment for RL's value. Transcribed as 'RRHF' in captions, which is almost certainly RLHF. ([2:47](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=167))
- **Galactica framed reasoning as internal thinking inside tags, before the 'thinking models' wave** — Beyond chain-of-thought prompting and scratchpads, Galactica treated intermediate reasoning as internal working memory placed inside special tags, with inference compute spent before the answer. The speaker says this was buried in the paper, and that his early idea was to apply RL pressure to the content inside those tags. He says DeepSeek-R1 did something similar about two years later. ([5:52](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=352))
- **A 2022-era PPO recipe got strong math results but no reflective, backtracking behaviour** — Recipe: continue pretraining Llama 2 on math and science data, then run PPO with verifiable rewards, with the value model initialized from an outcome reward model. It reached state of the art internally on math and reasoning but did not show the reflection that later defined R1 and o1. The speaker's explanation is that better base models and more RL compute, plus bigger context windows, are what let that behaviour emerge. He calls this the bitter lesson in its purest form. ([6:52](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=412))
- **Long horizon is a context problem: compaction plus RL on the same trajectory** — Hard research tasks may need tens or hundreds of billions of tokens of thinking, against a context window of about 1M tokens. Compaction (fill the window, summarize, keep going) is the proposed fix. RL is applied to the compaction step and the task together, so one signal trains both. ([10:15](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=615))
- **Long-horizon RL has three problems, and value models address them** — Gradient variance grows with trajectory length, rewards are sparse so credit assignment is hard, and trajectory lengths vary. A value model (critic) reduces variance, works at the trajectory level (which suits compaction), keeps batches diverse, and allows bootstrapping before the episode ends. The cost is a second model trained alongside the policy, which makes it more complicated than GRPO. ([10:15](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=615))
- **KellyBench: every frontier model lost money trading a season with $100K** — Agents build ML models to bet on Premier League matches over a one-year horizon, with $100K to start for each frontier model. All of them lost money. The speaker says the result drew unexpected mainstream attention, including a Financial Times front page. He ties it to a sports-betting market with real stakes, which Kaggle-style tasks lack. ([13:03](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=783))
- **Why models fail long horizons: narrow training distribution, closed-ended tasks, and no simulated players** — The speaker argues the industry is too biased toward coding and procedural tasks, which have one or two solutions and little room for creativity. Open-ended tasks are underrepresented, and real-world complexity (uncertainty, other agents with their own goals) is not simulated. ([13:03](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=783))
- **Pipeline RL trades off-policy lag against GPU utilization, and bootstrapping extends it** — Pipeline RL starts training on sequences that have finished while others are still generating, instead of waiting for the whole batch. In their experience, off-policy lag up to about 8 steps is acceptable. When inference takes weeks, the GPUs would sit idle past that bound, so the value model lets them train mid-episode. This adds value-model bias, so it is another trade-off. ([14:01](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=841))
- **OpenReward: environment infrastructure as a hosted service** — The speakers' platform hosts over 350 environments behind a single API endpoint. They use it for their own RL and say some frontier labs and new labs use it too. Presented briefly as a product plug near the end. ([16:27](https://youtu.be/2bvtay8wGYI?t=987))

## Techniques
- **Value-model (critic) RL for long, variable-length trajectories** — Train a value model alongside the policy. Use it to reduce gradient variance on long episodes and to give trajectory-level credit. Use its estimates to bootstrap expected return before the episode ends, so there is training signal while the episode is still running. Expect a second model to train and some value-model bias. *When:* Episodes are long, variable in length and have sparse rewards, as in multi-day or multi-week agent runs. The speaker prefers this to GRPO here, at the cost of complexity.
- **RL-trained compaction** — When the context window fills, have the model generate a summary and continue from it. Apply the same RL signal to the summary and to the overall task, so both are optimized together, with the value model providing trajectory-level credit. *When:* A task needs more reasoning or working memory than one context window holds, and the agent should learn what to keep in its summary.
- **File-system scratchpad, self-search and archive tools for long-horizon context** — Give the agent a file-system scratchpad for written reasoning, a tool to search its previous trajectory, and an archive of earlier results to build on. Watch for shortcuts: the agent should not just copy a previous answer without reasoning. *When:* Long runs where the agent has to revisit its own earlier work after context compaction or a restart.
- **Pipeline RL with bounded off-policy lag** — Run gradient updates on finished sequences while later sequences are still generating, instead of waiting for the whole batch. Cap how many steps behind the policy the data may be (about 8 steps, by their experience). For rollouts that exceed the cap, bootstrap with a value model rather than letting GPUs sit idle. *When:* GPU utilization is the bottleneck and rollouts are long. The speaker frames it as a trade-off between staleness and utilization.
- **Verifiable-reward PPO after continued pretraining (2022 recipe)** — Step 1: continue pretraining the base model on target-domain data (math and science). Step 2: run PPO with verifiable outcome rewards. Step 3: initialize the value model from an outcome reward model rather than from scratch. *When:* The domain has checkable answers, such as math or code tests. The speaker reports strong results with this recipe on 2022-era base models.
- **Open-ended, outcome-scored long-horizon benchmark** — Give each agent a fixed budget and a long horizon in a real-money-style domain, such as a one-year betting or trading task, and score by realized outcome instead of pass/fail tests. *When:* Evaluating agents on open-ended, long-horizon work where coding benchmarks saturate or do not measure the right thing.

## Hot takes
- Long-horizon RL is mainly a mindset and a context-management problem, not just an engineering one (speaker's framing, 9:06).
- The industry is too biased toward coding and procedural tasks, and benchmarks need open-ended, multi-agent, real-world complexity (13:03-14:01). The speakers' own benchmark and environment platform are the proposed remedy, so there is a vendor angle.
- Better base models and more RL compute explain why the 2022 PPO recipe did not show reflection. This is a post-hoc retrospective with no ablation shown.
- The pipeline RL bound of about 8 steps and the value-model bootstrapping results are one team's internal experience, with no curves or ablations on screen. Treat them as anecdotal.
- The KellyBench headline ('all lost money') comes with no model names, run counts or methodology. It is an attention-grabbing result, not a reproducible measurement.
