## Summary
This is a 58-minute audience Q&A with swyx, an AI Engineer organizer, held after the World's Fair. Attendees raised topics such as an AI-safety track, expo hours, Wi-Fi, workshop registration, the India and China chapters, meetups, and the mobile app. swyx says the event will stay the same size and venue next year and will optimize rather than grow. He describes a TEDx-style partner model for new cities and countries. He also explains that talk selection is competitive and not blind, and that prior recorded talks help applicants. The app feedback is detailed and was sent to his team as requirements. The session has almost no technical AI engineering content, so it is useful mainly as event-organizing context.

## Key ideas
- **No AI-safety or x-risk track, because the event is for work people can use right away.** — swyx draws the line at practical engineering. Safety content still appears in security, sandboxing, guardrails and eval tracks. Meetups are the place for other topics. ([1:20](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=80))
- **Next year the event stays the same size, venue and week.** — The goal is to optimize the existing space for a sustainable, long-running conference, not to chase growth. Expansion will come through new cities and countries. ([17:25](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=1045))
- **Cities and countries run their own events under a TEDx-style partner program.** — About 40 countries have expressed interest. swyx is willing to let partners run events as long as the brand still stands for good technical content. Bangalore is the likely first Indian city. ([9:03](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=543))
- **Talk selection is very competitive and applications are not blind.** — There were about 2,200 applications for roughly 300 slots, and some invited speakers are added on top. Reviewers watch prior talks, so recorded talks from meetups or other conferences help applicants. ([52:33](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=3153))
- **Attendee event apps are weak, so the organizers open-source their conference data.** — swyx says the JSON data was public, so people could build their own app or use an MCP or CLI. The team plans to replace the vendor app next year and will use the feedback as requirements. ([27:35](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=1655))
- **Workshops are kept short on purpose because online attention is shrinking.** — Most content is 20-minute talks, with separate longer workshop rooms. Attendees said 20 minutes is too short for depth, and the reply was that the trade-off is intentional. ([29:44](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=1784))
- **Sequencing talks for a YouTube playlist matters more than the live schedule.** — Speakers have conflicts, so the organizers cluster topics where they can. They also build one playlist per track, although few people use them. ([32:32](https://youtu.be/snTZtttfL70?t=1952))

## Techniques
- **Publish conference data as open JSON** — Expose the schedule and speaker data publicly so attendees can build their own apps or query it through MCP or CLI, instead of relying on one vendor app. *When:* When you run an event or internal conference with a tight budget and a weak vendor app.
- **Partner-run local meetups with a fixed cadence** — Keep the same venue and day each month, let volunteers share the work, and treat the core deliverable as event organization plus publishing recordings. Skip heavy production. *When:* When starting a community chapter or meetup series that needs to stay low-effort.

## Hot takes
- Most conference apps are bad, so publishing the raw event data lets attendees build better ones.
- Short 20-minute talks suit YouTube attention spans better than long workshops.
- Keeping the event the same size and venue is a deliberate way to make it sustainable.
