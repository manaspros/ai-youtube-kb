> Cursor's forward deployed engineering lead on where FDE fits, whom to hire, and how to scope short, ROI-driven customer projects without drifting into staff augmentation.

## Summary
Pauline Vernet, who leads Cursor's global forward deployed engineering (FDE) team, argues that FDE is not professional services or staff augmentation and should be used only where it creates lasting value. She frames the choice on a 2x2 of customer digital maturity against product customization: mature, low-customization customers should self-serve, and low-maturity, low-customization customers get a traditional SaaS rollout. FDE fits best where customers are mature and the product is highly customizable, and in embedded transformation for less mature customers. Her hiring model is senior, highly technical engineers (five or more years) with customer-facing skill, organized by geography for now and expected to move toward industries. Engagements should be scoped as directional, roughly six-week phases tied to a measurable baseline, with the customer owning every step, and the team should measure ROI as revenue, cost, or risk. She also warns against red flags such as requests to "fill a staffing gap" and advises leaving documentation artifacts behind and partnering with system integrators for change management.

## Key ideas
- **Place the customer on a maturity vs. customization matrix before deciding whether FDE is the right motion.** — Mature customers with low customization should self-serve, and low-maturity, low-customization customers are a traditional SaaS deployment. FDE fits high-maturity, highly customizable products (acting as advisors), and embedded transformation for less mature customers who need hands-on help. ([1:28](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=88))
- **FDE is not staff augmentation or product training, and it should not be used to fill customer staffing gaps.** — She calls a request to 'we're understaffed, do this' a red flag. Her test is to ask who on the customer side will work alongside the team. She also says FDE should not run 101/201 product workshops for the whole company. ([9:52](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=592))
- **Hire senior, technical people with customer skills, and avoid early-career hires for now.** — Cursor hires engineers with five-plus years of experience and customer-facing aptitude. Later the roles may split into more customer-facing and more technical tracks, and the team may organize by industry, since using an industry's vocabulary builds credibility. ([11:12](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=672))
- **Scope engagements as directional, time-boxed projects tied to a measurable KPI, not open-ended staffing.** — Define success up front with a baseline and a target, for example an automated process taking 20 minutes instead of a 3-hour baseline. Scope in about six-week phases so the team can pivot as they learn, since they have not yet seen the customer's data or systems. ([16:14](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=974))
- **The customer must own the project, with the FDE team working side by side in their code base.** — She expects customers to be involved in scoping, design, implementation, human-in-the-loop validation, and baseline-vs-result comparison. If the FDE works alone in a corner of the customer's office, she sees that as a problem. ([17:28](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=1048))
- **Frame every project's ROI as revenue increase, cost reduction, or risk mitigation, and measure it.** — She gives an example of an agent costing $2,000 a day that was justified once the team tied it to sending the right technician to fix equipment. Customers often do not measure value this way until someone asks. ([18:53](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=1133))
- **Use the FDE team as a feedback loop into product and engineering, and tell customers when they can say no.** — Customer requests that Cursor is not built for are answered with honesty, which builds trust and surfaces other use cases. Frequent repeated requests are passed to product as roadmap input, and edge use cases outside the SDLC are tested first with FDE. ([13:39](https://youtu.be/APqXGyCoGW4?t=819))

## Techniques
- **FDE placement matrix** — Plot each customer on digital maturity (x) against product customization (y). Self-serve the mature, low-customization quadrant, run traditional SaaS for low-maturity, low-customization accounts, and reserve FDE for the high-customization and embedded-transformation zones. *When:* Deciding whether a customer deserves dedicated forward deployed engineers or a self-service path.
- **Staff-augmentation check** — When a customer asks for help because they are understaffed, ask who on their team will work with you. If nobody can be named, treat it as a red flag and decline or restructure. *When:* Screening engagements before committing FDE capacity.
- **Success defined before build** — Agree with the business owner on a baseline metric and a target (for example, 3 hours to 20 minutes), then verify against the baseline with the customer in human-in-the-loop validation. *When:* Starting any customer automation or agent project.
- **Directional six-week phased scope** — Scope the outcome, not the headcount. Commit to phase one and two over about six weeks, and be ready to pivot as the team learns the data and systems. *When:* Structuring an FDE engagement with unknown customer processes.
- **Three-bucket ROI statement** — Describe each project as increasing revenue, decreasing cost, or mitigating risk, and record the comparison against baseline so the customer can justify spend such as a costly agent. *When:* Reporting ROI to a customer or justifying an agent's running cost.
- **Mission statement for the FDE team** — Write one clear sentence of what the team drives for the customer and use it to decide whether a project fits or looks like staff augmentation. *When:* Setting up or steering an FDE function.

## Hot takes
- FDE is not professional services or staff augmentation, and a 'we are understaffed' request is a red flag.
- Do not run product 101/201 training or workshops for the whole company as an FDE.
- Do not hire early-career engineers for FDE work at this stage; hire senior, technical people.
- Do not take on FDE projects without a named counterpart team and a measurable ROI.
- Saying no to a use case the product is not built for builds more trust than saying yes.
