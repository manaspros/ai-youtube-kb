## Summary
Swix, a co-founder of AI Engineer, gives a 17-minute behind-the-scenes update on the 2026 World's Fair. He says it is the largest AIE yet, with about half the topics kept from earlier years and half new, including auto research, a split of GPU content into inference, post-training and pre-training, and memory and continual learning. He pitches the expo floor and hallway conversations as the main value, and an Explorer ticket as the cheapest way to get floor access. He describes new formats: poster sessions for printed tweets and blog posts, a leadership track with a "token billionaire" lounge, daily themes, and off-the-record McKinsey sessions. The show is shifting from horizontal tracks to verticals (forward-deployed engineering, agentic commerce, healthcare, finance, GTM), with an AI and finance New York event announced. He closes by listing side events and a new-attendee orientation (NEO). The video is logistics and promotion with no technical method or evidence.

## Key ideas
- **The conference is pitched as a curated buffet, and one ticket covers a selection of tracks.** — The speaker says the 2026 show is larger than all previous AIEs combined and that size itself is not the appeal. The point is that one ticket covers a curated mix of content. ([1:29](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=89))
- **About half of the topics are kept and half are new.** — New top tracks include auto research, which did not exist last year. GPU content is split into inference, post-training and pre-training, and a data-quality track is added. Memory and continual learning are new as well. ([2:49](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=169))
- **The expo floor and hallway conversations are presented as the main value, more than the talks.** — Talks are recorded anyway, so the speaker says attendees should spend time on the expo floor with four expo stages. An Explorer ticket, the cheapest tier, gives floor access. ([3:49](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=229))
- **Poster sessions are being redesigned to cover more than papers.** — The speaker says papers are often months out of date by the time they reach the floor. He puts out a call for printed tweets, blog posts, and products to be defended at poster sessions, on the view that there is something to learn from them too. ([7:35](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=455))
- **The leadership track is a large share of the breakout space, with a token-spend lounge.** — Level 3 is reserved for leadership, about 50% of the breakout floor. The speaker describes a token billionaire program for very heavy token spenders, a lounge for conversations about spending more or less. ([9:02](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=542))
- **Verticals are replacing horizontal tracks as the main organizing idea.** — This year's tracks are forward-deployed engineering, agentic commerce, healthcare, finance and GTM. The speaker argues finance is the closest vertical to takeoff after code, and announces AI Engineer New York as a finance-focused event. ([11:27](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=687))
- **Daily themes and off-the-record networking are new.** — Themes are set by day and cover real workflows from non-vendors, token maxing and reduction, and building software factories for companies. McKinsey runs a daily off-the-record session, and a networking room offers meetings with speakers. ([10:27](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=627))
- **A new-engineer orientation targets solo attendees.** — NEO is an orientation the night before day one for people attending alone. About 300 people had signed up at recording, and the speaker expects around 1,000. ([14:14](https://youtu.be/0S8xe9ftGTM?t=854))
