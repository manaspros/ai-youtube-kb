> OpenAI's Joseph Kim explains how ChatGPT for Financial Services was built on the plugin platform (MCP, skills, artifact tools), why finance lagged in AI use, and his 2027 bets on finance models and persistent agents.

## Summary
Joseph Kim, who leads ChatGPT for Financial Services at OpenAI, presents the product launched about a month earlier with design partners Morgan Stanley and Evercore, focused on investment banking and equity research. He frames the gap between model capability and day-to-day value as a "capability overhang" and cites internal Codex usage data showing finance, legal and recruiting went from almost no Codex-generated output in early 2026 to about 90% by June, with the inflection tied to the Codex desktop app rather than a new model. On the build side, the team licensed financial data and hosted and indexed it in-house because existing MCP connectors had latency, error and search-quality problems, still exposing it to the model over MCP. Excel/PowerPoint outputs use firm templates, with citation viewers and interactive charts for review. The talk then covers the "threshold of usefulness" idea for when a task becomes worth delegating, the plan for finance-specific post-training over the next three months, and a prediction that persistent agents are the next form of unhobbling. The content is a vendor talk with some product pitch; the forecasts (for example, models matching top experts in 2027 and $1B businesses built as plugins) are the speaker's expectations, not evidence.

## Key ideas
- **Capability overhang: the gap between what models can do and the value people actually get in daily work. Closing it needs data, tools and an experience that fits the work, not just a better model.** — Speaker's framing for why ChatGPT for Financial Services exists. He points to the Codex desktop app as the product change that moved non-engineers, not a new model. ([4:16](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=256))
- **Internal Codex usage: finance, legal and recruiting went from almost no AI output generated in Codex in early 2026 to about 90% by June. The inflection came around February-March, when the Codex desktop app shipped.** — Claimed from OpenAI internal chart with no methodology shown. The takeaway is that product packaging, not model release, drove adoption outside engineering. ([5:18](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=318))
- **Data first: the product's value is capped by source quality. They licensed financial data (statements, earnings transcripts, market and private-company data) instead of relying on third-party MCP connectors.** — Existing financial MCP connectors had latency, error and search-quality issues. OpenAI hosts and indexes the data on the same infrastructure as its web index, still serves it through MCP, and can post-train on the datasets. ([5:18](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=318))
- **MCP plus skills gives the model access and instructions (like a junior analyst's manual). Post-training on the data gives familiarity with its quirks (like a senior analyst). Both are used.** — Analogy the speaker uses to separate tool/instruction layers from training on the domain data. ([6:45](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=405))
- **Output fit matters as much as answers: firm-approved templates for Excel and PowerPoint, interactive financial charts, and citation viewers that show the source passages and tables behind a number.** — Reviewability is treated as part of the UX: users need to check where a figure came from and its context. ([6:45](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=405))
- **The product is built entirely on the ChatGPT plugin platform: MCP tools and connectors for data, citation metadata and MCP apps for source inspection and charts, skills plus shared artifact tools for Excel and PowerPoint, and native plugin onboarding.** — Speaker's claim that the same building blocks are open to developers, and that multiple $1B businesses will be built as ChatGPT plugins. The $1B claim is a forecast. ([8:09](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=489))
- **Three drivers of model progress: scaling compute, algorithmic efficiency, and unhobbling (getting more useful work out of models). Algorithmic compute needed for a fixed performance level is said to halve about every eight months.** — Speaker cites scaling-law predictions (e.g., predicting a model's loss before training finished) and synthetic data and verifiable rewards as answers to the data wall. ([9:36](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=576))
- **Threshold phenomenon: a task stays unhelpful until the model is reliable enough that checking its work takes much less time than doing it yourself. Then adoption jumps even when the underlying improvement is similar.** — Product advice: list capabilities below the threshold, ask where they will be in a year, and design the interface now for delegation (what the user still controls, reviews or approves). ([13:02](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=782))
- **Finance progress comes from two forces: the rising tide (general model improvement) and waves (finance-specific post-training). Next three months: financial reasoning, Excel modelling and PowerPoint generation for junior-analyst tasks.** — Longer-term target: match the best human experts on forecasting earnings and investment judgment sometime in 2027. This is a stated goal, not a result. ([15:06](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=906))
- **Next form of unhobbling: proactive, persistent agents that hold a goal, remember context and keep working in the background, and that can be managed the way people manage colleagues (share a project, ask for an update).** — Speaker's bet on why persistence matters: it changes the interface and lowers the skill needed to use agents. ([16:32](https://youtu.be/opHmX7qClzE?t=992))

## Techniques
- **Host and index licensed data yourself, expose it through MCP** — License the source data, host and index it on your own infrastructure, and serve it to the model over MCP. Measure latency, errors and search quality before trusting a third-party connector. *When:* When existing MCP connectors for a data domain are unreliable or low quality and the product's value depends on the data.
- **Split tool/instruction layer from domain post-training** — Use MCP tools and skills to give the model access and step-by-step instructions. Use post-training on the domain data for familiarity with its quirks, since that is a separate lever. *When:* When the domain data is used repeatedly and the model needs to know its quirks, not just how to call a tool.
- **Firm templates for generated artifacts** — Give users access to approved organisation templates when the model generates spreadsheets and presentations, so outputs follow house formats. *When:* When outputs must match an organisation's standards before they reach a team or client.
- **Citation viewers for reviewable answers** — Attach citation metadata to answers and render the source passages and tables behind each figure, so a reviewer can check where a number came from. *When:* When users must verify numbers and context before using an answer, as in finance.
- **Plan features around the usefulness threshold** — List tasks that are underappreciated because the model is not yet reliable enough. For each, design the interface now: what the user controls, reviews or approves before delegating. *When:* When planning the roadmap for agent features that are not yet reliable enough to delegate.
- **Build on the plugin platform building blocks** — Combine MCP tools for data, MCP apps for interactive views, skills with shared artifact tools for document output, and native plugin onboarding to ship a domain product on top of the host platform. *When:* When you have unique data or a deep workflow and want to ship it inside a host assistant rather than building the whole UI yourself.

## Hot takes
- Vendor talk: most of the 'how we built it' section is a pitch for the ChatGPT plugin platform, with no independent evidence of finance quality or accuracy.
- The Codex-share chart (about 90% by June) is internal OpenAI data with no methodology or denominator, and the speaker attributes the inflection to the desktop app with no ablation.
- Forecasts such as models beating top finance experts by 2027, Excel and PowerPoint being rarely used by 2027, and multiple $1B plugin businesses are speaker bets, not results.
- The Navier-Stokes and conjecture claims about internal models are unverified in the talk. Model names in the captions are garbled, so treat specific model references as unreliable.
- The strongest practitioner point is the one-time data decision: third-party MCP financial connectors were unreliable, so OpenAI licensed and hosted the data itself and kept MCP only as the access layer.

## Relevance to our work
- **general** (2/3): The threshold-of-usefulness framing and the advice to design the interface for delegation (what the user controls, reviews or approves) apply to any agent product plan. → *For each agent feature on the roadmap, write down the review and approval step needed before the task counts as delegable, then design that step first.*

This maps onto the MemoryMesh question of retrieval versus learned familiarity, but the talk gives no retrieval-quality data. → *Note the MCP/skills vs post-training split when deciding whether MemoryMesh knowledge buckets should be served only by retrieval or also by fine-tuned domain models.*
