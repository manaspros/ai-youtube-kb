> Arca, an AI-native financial services firm, explains how background agents own multi-day client work through a signal system, shared plan spaces, and human pull-ins.

## Summary
Aman Singh, who leads AI at Arca, describes an AI-native financial services firm where agents execute the decisions behind client outcomes (financial, tax and investment planning) rather than assisting from the sidelines. He frames the work as long-horizon but stop-start: a single client goal can span about 10 days with many handoffs, such as a tax-raising plan that waits on asset settlement and an ACH transfer. Arca's approach is to take humans off the critical path. Background agents continuously read external signals (emails, calls, custodian events, market moves), act on them with operational context, persistent client memory, a harness with tools and guardrails, and a sandbox for financial computation. Agents post plans into a shared per-goal space where people chat with them and get pulled in as tasks when their input is needed. Agents register interest in events, get woken by signals (including human actions in the UI) or by their own schedule, and notify each other. He says the team spent six months closing context gaps, tuning task precision, and collecting human reasoning as evals. The talk ends with an example of adding a new goal (proactive January tax outreach) so agents generate one-click approval plans. The talk has no benchmarks, failure rates, or implementation details, and it closes with a hiring pitch.

- **The target workload is long-horizon but stop-start, not long-running token grinding.** — One client goal spans about 10 days with many actors, decision points and handoffs. The example is a client with a large April 15 tax bill whose assets take days to settle and whose ACH transfer takes days to land. ([1:07](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=67))
- **Background agents watch a signal stream and act on it continuously.** — Phone calls, documents, meetings, custodian events and market movement are all signals. Agents evaluate them continuously to advance work, and an incoming email is one such signal. ([4:29](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=269))
- **Agents register interest in future events and get routed the signals they care about.** — An agent can say 'let me know when Jane responds' or 'when the ACH lands'. A signal system routes events to interested agents, and agents can also notify other agents. ([5:49](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=349))
- **Human actions inside the product are signals too.** — Rescheduling a calendar event, marking a task complete, or entering new facts from an in-person meeting all wake agents, so the agents react to the internal world as well as the external one. ([5:49](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=349))
- **Agents self-wake on a schedule when an expected signal never arrives.** — The speaker's example is a client who never replies to an email. The agent falls back to a timed check to keep the work moving. ([5:49](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=349))
- **Each goal gets a shared space where the plan, people and agents meet.** — Agents post the plan there, several people can chat with the agent about it, and the agent pulls in the right person as a task when a human has to execute a step. Approving the plan triggers the human-executed steps. ([4:29](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=269))
- **The speaker's stated primitives are awareness, operational context, a harness with persistent memory, and human collaboration.** — Signals give awareness, operational context says what to do, the harness gives tools, guardrails and state across the long horizon, and the collaboration layer brings people in. Work that must be tracked for later is kept in agent memory, and work doable now becomes a task. ([8:40](https://youtu.be/oP2WLFpMoDY?t=520))
- **Human reasoning from daily use is captured as eval data, and the company said it spent six months on context gaps and task precision.** — Because staff use the product, their recorded reasoning becomes evals for improving the system. ## Techniques
- **Signal-driven wake-up with interest registration** — Let agents declare interest in a named future event (a client reply, a settlement landing). Route each incoming signal from email, custodian feeds, calls, market data and UI events to every agent that registered for it. Agents can also message each other about a signal. The talk gives no implementation details such as the message bus, schema or delivery guarantees. *When:* Use for long waits with external actors, where a polling loop would waste tokens or miss the event. Relevant to AgentOS's event and signal layer and to ProsHarness's resume triggers.
- **Dual wake-up: event plus timed fallback** — Each waiting agent has a signal subscription and also a schedule. If the expected event does not arrive by the scheduled check, the agent wakes itself and re-evaluates the work. *When:* Use when the external party may never respond, so the workflow cannot rely on signals alone.
- **Treat UI mutations as first-class events** — Emit an event for every human action in the product (reschedule, task complete, note added) and feed it into the same signal system the background agents use, so agents see human progress without a separate sync step. *When:* Use when humans and agents share the same work state and agents must react to human edits.
- **One shared workspace per goal with human pull-ins** — Give each goal a space where the agent posts its plan, multiple people can chat with it, and the agent creates a task for a person when that person must act. Approving the plan releases the outbound steps, such as sending the email to the client. *When:* Use for multi-party approval flows, where a plan must be reviewed and then executed partly by people. This maps to ProsHarness's plan, critique and approval loop and its phone-driven approvals.
- **Split state by horizon: memory for later, tasks for now** — Work that must be tracked for the future goes into the agent's persistent memory. Work that can be done now becomes a human-facing task. The talk gives no storage format or retrieval method. *When:* Use to keep a long-running agent's state from becoming a to-do list, and to hold commitments such as 'do not touch this position' across months.
- **Turn colleagues' reasoning into eval cases** — Record the reasoning staff give while using the product (corrections, approvals, reasons for changes) and store it as evaluation data for the agents. *When:* Use when the product's day-to-day users are domain experts, and no labelled dataset exists yet.
- **Steer by adding a goal** — Add a new business goal (e.g. proactively prepare each client's tax situation every January) to the agent's goal set. The agents then produce the analyses and one-click approval plans themselves, without a new workflow being hand-built. *When:* Use when the business wants a new outcome that the existing signals and tools can serve, to see whether agents can drive it from goals alone.

## Hot takes
- The speaker claims 'agents run the business' and that you know a company is AI native when you can steer it by steering its agents. This is a framing, not evidence, and no outcome metric is given.
- The speaker says 'agents prompt people more than people prompt agents' as the goal, which is a vision claim with no measurement.
- The speaker says harnesses are converging on a standard shape and that the interesting work is outside the harness. This is a useful point from a practitioner, but unsupported by data here.
- The talk is a company showcase with a hiring pitch at the end. Substance is architectural outline, with no failure modes, latency, cost or accuracy figures and no code or config. Quality is capped by thin detail.
