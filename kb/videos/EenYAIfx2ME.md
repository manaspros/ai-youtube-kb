## Key ideas
- **SANA streaming: real-time video-to-video editing from text instructions, aimed at live broadcast and gaming, with a hybrid DiT that mixes softmax attention (some blocks) and linear attention (the rest) for bounded-memory streaming.** — Target is 1280x704 and the speaker says it runs in real time on one RTX 5090. Claim is minute-length editing with near-1080p-equivalent quality. The speaker presents this as a vendor claim from the paper. ([1:01](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=61))
- **Cycle reverse regularization is the trick for temporal consistency in SANA streaming: the model is trained to predict the original source frames from its own generated output.** — This ties generated frames back to the source so the edit does not drift over time. It is one of three pillars the speaker lists (architecture, this training strategy, and streaming with bounded memory). ([1:01](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=61))
- **Holo 3.1 (H Company) targets deployment flexibility for computer-use agents: mobile automation, function calling alongside JSON output, and local quantized inference.** — It ships FP8, Q4 GGUF and NVFP4 for the 35B model, its first quantized release. Model sizes are 0.8B, 4B, 9B and 35B-A3B. The speaker says the Q4 GGUF runs on consumer Mac or Windows hardware. ([3:30](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=210))
- **Dots.TTS (RedNote AI Lab) drops discrete audio tokens from the pipeline entirely.** — The design is a semantic encoder, an LLM, and an autoregressive flow-matching acoustic head over a 48 kHz audio VAE, so prediction happens in a continuous latent space. The speaker presents the reported Seed TTS eval WERs and calls it architecturally interesting. Apache 2.0 weights. ([6:13](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=373))
- **D4RT (Google DeepMind, CVPR 2026 best paper) replaces separate depth, tracking and camera-pose models with one model that does sparse 3D tracking, depth reconstruction and all-pixel 3D tracking.** — The all-pixel mode produces a holistic 4D scene reconstruction in world coordinates. Interactive 4D reconstructions are on the project page. ([7:38](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=458))
- **Tanker is pitched as GitHub for robotics, bundling hardware (BOM and assembly steps, linked to Onshape and SolidWorks), software, datasets and VLA model deployment in one place.** — Its standout feature, Leonardo, reads first-person build video and generates step-by-step assembly docs that other builders can ask questions about. The speaker presents this as a launch pitch and did not test it. ([10:08](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=608))
- **MAMA (Max Planck, CVPR 2026 oral) is markerless multi-person mocap built for close contact. Its model predicts uncertainty, visibility and contact probabilities, and a contact optimization step stops bodies from interpenetrating.** — MamaNet is a transformer dense-landmark estimator. MamaSyn is a synthetic dataset of 2.5M crops extending BEDLAM to multi-view. Code and data are on GitHub. ([11:27](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=687))
- **QJudger (Qwen Image Bench) is an open judge model built on Qwen 3.5 that scores generated images on five dimensions and aggregates them. It runs locally with no closed-source API.** — Dimensions are quality, aesthetics, alignment, real-world fidelity and creative generation. Raw scores are mapped to a normalized scale and then aggregated. The paper reports high human agreement. Apache 2.0. ([14:18](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=858))
- **VibeVoice (Microsoft) generates up to 90 minutes of audio with up to four speakers, using ultra-low-frame-rate 7.5 Hz continuous speech tokenizers.** — An LLM handles dialogue context and a diffusion head generates the acoustic detail. The speaker says the demo clips include 45- and 100-minute podcasts and cross-lingual voice transfer. ([15:42](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=942))
- **Safety caveat: Microsoft says it disabled the VibeVoice GitHub repo after finding misuse, including impersonation, that conflicted with its research intent. The demo page remains up.** — The speaker reports this from Microsoft's GitHub notice and includes it as a flag on the release. ([17:07](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=1027))
- **Humanoid GPT (Tsinghua and Galbot, CVPR 2026) targets the agility-generalization trade-off in humanoid motion tracking. Its answer is scale: a 2B-frame motion corpus and a GPT-style transformer trained with DAgger distillation from motion experts.** — The baseline Sonic was trained on about 100M frames, so this is roughly 20x more data. It runs on a Unitree G1, with end-to-end latency under 1.5 ms on an RTX 4090 using ONNX and TensorRT. The speaker says the paper shows scaling laws for both data and model size. ([18:22](https://youtu.be/EenYAIfx2ME?t=1102))

## Techniques
- **Hybrid softmax/linear attention diffusion transformer for streaming video** — Keep softmax attention in some blocks for better local modeling and use linear attention in the remaining blocks for throughput. The paper frames this as what makes bounded-memory, continuously running streaming possible. *When:* Per-frame generation or editing that has to run continuously at a fixed cost, such as live video, rather than rendering a whole clip in batch.
- **Cycle reverse regularization for temporal consistency** — Train the generator so it can predict the original source frames back from its own generated output. This forces generated content to stay semantically tied to the source across time. *When:* Video-to-video editing or any long generation where per-frame quality is fine but identity or content drifts over time.
- **Codebook-free autoregressive TTS with a flow-matching head** — Use a semantic encoder and an LLM for context, then an autoregressive flow-matching head that predicts continuous latents from a high-rate audio VAE. No discrete audio token prediction and no codec decode step. *When:* When quantization loss in discrete audio codecs is the limiting factor on naturalness or artifacts.
- **Ultra-low frame-rate continuous speech tokenizers for long-form dialogue** — Tokenize speech at about 7.5 Hz with continuous acoustic and semantic tokenizers, so the LLM sees a short sequence. Add a diffusion head for high-fidelity acoustic detail. *When:* Generating long multi-speaker audio where sequence length, not model quality, is the main compute cost.
- **Multi-dimension structured LLM-as-judge with human-agreement validation** — Score each generated output on fixed dimensions with a structured rubric. Map raw scores to a normalized scale, aggregate them, then check agreement against human ratings from the paper. The judge runs locally. *When:* Evaluating generative outputs without a closed-source judge API. Could support local eval pipelines such as RAGAS-style checks.
- **Contact-aware optimization for close multi-person pose** — Predict per-landmark uncertainty and visibility, plus person-to-person and floor contact probabilities. In optimization, penalize body interpenetration when contact is predicted and encourage plausible contact geometry. *When:* Motion capture of closely interacting people, where occlusion and body overlap break per-frame estimators.
- **DAgger distillation from motion experts into a GPT-style controller** — Train a transformer policy with DAgger from motion-tracking experts over a very large, aggregated motion corpus, then optimize inference with ONNX and TensorRT. *When:* Training one controller that covers both agile and unseen motions, and needs low real-time latency on the robot.
- **Compare quantized formats on your own hardware before choosing one** — The release ships FP8, Q4 GGUF and NVFP4 weights. The speaker reports NVFP4 at 1.41x the token throughput of FP8 and 1.74x of BF16 on DGX Spark, with harness optimizations. Check the same comparison on your own GPU before adopting a format. *When:* Picking a local serving format for an agent model where throughput per step matters more than the last bit of accuracy.

## Hot takes
- The whole episode is a link-driven digest. Almost every benchmark and capability claim is repeated from the project page with no independent check, and the speaker does not test any tool. Treat the numbers as vendor-reported.
- The title and opener ("AI broke the internet again", "absolutely stacked") are hype framing for a list of releases. Several items (Tanker, Leonardo) are presented as launch pitches with no evidence beyond the demo page.
- The VibeVoice misuse disclosure (Microsoft disabled the repo after impersonation and other misuse) is the most consequential fact in the episode for anyone planning to adopt a voice-cloning or long-form TTS model.
- Holo 3.1's NVFP4 speedup is the only item with a concrete deployment number. It comes from DGX Spark and includes harness optimizations, so it does not isolate the quantization effect.
- The scaling-laws claim in Humanoid GPT is stated, but the speaker only restates the paper's framing.
