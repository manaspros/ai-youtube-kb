> A go-to-market talk for developer-tool founders: distribution is now the bottleneck, so use a signal-vs-revenue check, a six-step GTM routine, and personal brand to get customers.

## Summary
Victoria Melnikova of Evil Martians argues that building software is now easy, so distribution is the bottleneck for developer tools in 2026. She presents the PMF Compass, a two-axis check comparing product signal with revenue, which she says shows distribution is the weak point for about nine in ten early-stage dev tool startups. She then walks through six basic go-to-market steps: validate the value proposition with paying customers, understand where developers spend time, pick channels, become the authority on the problem, work with the ecosystem, and distribute early. Much of the talk is about San Francisco as a network hub, with billboards, events and user conferences as signaling tools. She closes by arguing that founder personal brand and craft are the moat, since AI can amplify a person's signal but not replace it. The talk gives almost no hard data; the framework's underlying analysis is not shown.

## Key ideas
- **Distribution, not building, is the bottleneck for dev tools now** — Speaker says AI made software easy to build, so the constraint has moved to getting noticed and sold. ([1:21](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=81))
- **PMF Compass: compare product signal against revenue to decide what to fix** — If signal is stronger than revenue, work on distribution; if revenue is stronger, work on the product; if they match, go full speed. The speaker says nine in ten early-stage dev tool startups have signal stronger than revenue. The framework came from analysing 37 successful dev tools. ([1:21](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=81))
- **Six-step GTM hygiene before scaling spend** — Crystallise the value proposition and test it with paying customers; map where developers spend time; choose channels; become the authority on the problem; work with ecosystem partners and know competitors; distribute before the product is ready. ([2:52](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=172))
- **Be physically present where the buyers and investors are** — The speaker argues SF is a network game for founders and fundraising, and that being in person speeds up access to clients and VCs. ([4:08](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=248))
- **Visible spend is a credibility signal for a company without funding milestones** — Speaker says Typesense, not VC-backed, used a run of SF billboards as a proxy for trajectory; people then said they did not realise the company was that big. ([5:05](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=305))
- **Failure stories and quirks build trust** — Speaker says polished brands are boring; a recovered failure and a founder's 'genius zone' make the brand memorable. ([9:07](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=547))
- **Founder-led personal brand is the moat, and AI should amplify it** — Speaker says founder-led sales builds trust, that AI can amplify a person's signal across text and video, and that AI cannot replace a unique brand. ([10:06](https://youtu.be/G6IlDzj8OjA?t=606))

## Techniques
- **PMF Compass diagnostic** — Score product signal and revenue on two axes; if signal exceeds revenue, put effort into distribution; if revenue exceeds signal, put effort into the product; if equal, proceed. *When:* Early-stage dev tool company deciding whether its next quarter should go to product or distribution work.
- **Six-step GTM hygiene checklist** — Validate value prop with paying customers, map developer shelf space and channels, become the authority on the problem, map ecosystem friends and foes, and start distributing before launch. *When:* Before spending on marketing or events, or before a launch.
- **Founder-as-channel distribution** — The founder personally posts and talks to prospects (speaker cites a PlanetScale CEO who says many customers come from his own social account) and does founder-led sales. *When:* Early-stage startup with a founder who is comfortable being public and cannot yet delegate sales.
- **Visible, low-cost proof of momentum** — Use billboards, banners, meetups, or small events as a stand-in for funding milestones to signal that the company exists and is growing. *When:* Company without VC milestones that needs credibility with buyers and investors.

## Hot takes
- Your personal brand is the moat; founders who build it will stand out in 2026 and 2030.
- AI cannot replace the unique brand of a person; it can only amplify it.
- Once you are in SF, you have to get loud: banners on buses and highways are a credibility signal.
- Perfect is boring; people love a good failure story with an elegant recovery.
