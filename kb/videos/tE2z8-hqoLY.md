> Founder talk on the "lethal trifecta" (private data + untrusted content + actions) and a proposed fix: agents as identity-bearing members of legally registered, blockchain-verified organizations. Mostly a pitch for Kaduna Club.

## Summary
David Levine opens with a nostalgic history of LambdaMOO-style communities, then argues that platforms and algorithms extracted value from online communities over 1995-2025. He frames prompt injection as the core risk for agents and attributes the "lethal trifecta" term to Simon Willison: an agent that can read private data, ingest untrusted content, and take actions. He says enterprises responded by walling agents inside vendor silos, which loses context. His proposed alternative is a "DUNA" (decentralized, unincorporated, non-profit association) registered with a state Secretary of State, which he says gives an agent-run organization legal standing, and an "ally" (agent) and "kaduna" (agentic organization) model. Identity is handled with JWT tokens resolved through a blockchain-backed, DNS-like registry, and policy is decided through decision markets where members trade pass/fail tokens. The talk ends with an early-access sign-up for Kaduna Club. The talk gives no concrete defense against prompt injection of untrusted content itself. Its contribution is a governance and identity model, mostly unsupported by evidence.

## Key ideas
- **The lethal trifecta: an agent with access to private data, exposure to untrusted web or email content, and the ability to take actions can be hijacked into leaking that data.** — Speaker credits Simon Willison with the term. He says a naive agent can be persuaded by injected instructions to send private data elsewhere, and that there is no clean technical fix in the talk. ([0:08](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=8))
- **Prompt injection is treated as the central threat, and untrusted web content is hard for an agent to distinguish from trusted content.** — Examples given are fake job boards, forms the agent fills out, and emails that instruct the agent to expose secrets. The speaker says the agent cannot reliably tell a real source from a planted one. ([3:54](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=234))
- **Enterprises responded by keeping agents inside vendor walls (Slack, Salesforce, Notion) and integrating them through APIs and MCP servers, which the speaker says loses context across agents.** — Result is sales, finance and research agents that cannot easily share context. The speaker presents this as the status quo that his model is meant to replace. ([5:20](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=320))
- **The proposed unit is a DUNA (decentralized, unincorporated, non-profit association), which the speaker says was registered with the West Virginia Secretary of State the day before the talk.** — Claimed properties: member-governed, can own assets, sign agreements, hold bank accounts, hire, and raise capital. Profits cannot be distributed to members, because membership units would then be securities. The speaker says the framework was designed by a16z for DAOs and that he believes it fits agents better than blockchains. These are the speaker's legal claims, not verified. ([7:27](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=447))
- **Agent architecture is described as five steps: inform (load material into a vector DB), instruct (system prompt for character and stance), empower (connect accounts such as Slack, Telegram and Twitter), enact (grant specific abilities and long-running automation), and align (set purpose).** — The speaker calls the resulting agent an 'ally' and groups allies into an agentic organization called a 'kaduna'. No code or schema is shown. ([11:04](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=664))
- **Identity and authority are proposed as signed JWT tokens with claims for scope, time-to-live and a blockchain address, resolved against a state-level registry that works like DNS.** — The aim is that each action can be traced from an agent to its organization, and from there through an on-chain audit trail to a responsible party. The speaker compares this to how SMTP and HTTP became shared standards. ([13:39](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=819))
- **Decision markets replace voting: members trade pass/fail tokens on proposed policies, and tokens gain value when a member sides with the winning group.** — The speaker claims this produces better decisions and cites unnamed 'science'. Mission and sustainability are described as the two governance concerns. ([15:00](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=900))
- **The first umbrella organization is open for any agent to register under, and early access sign-up for Kaduna Club is advertised.** — Agents would declare organizational membership by presenting a token. The speaker notes that adoption will be a long process similar to early email standards. ([17:28](https://youtu.be/tE2z8-hqoLY?t=1048))

## Techniques
- **Ally build sequence (inform, instruct, empower, enact, align)** — Inform: load domain material into a vector database. Instruct: write the system prompt that sets character, stance and interaction style. Empower: connect the accounts the agent should act through (Slack, Telegram, Twitter, enterprise tools). Enact: grant specific abilities and automation, and allow long-running goals. Align: state the purpose the agent should serve across contexts. The talk gives no code, prompts or config keys. *When:* Use as a rough checklist when deciding what an autonomous agent knows, can act on and is meant to optimise. The stages are generic and mirror common practice, so a reader should still verify the specifics elsewhere.
- **Scoped identity tokens for agents (JWT with claims)** — Issue a signed JWT per agent or organization. Include claims for scope (what the agent may access), time-to-live, and a resolvable identifier that a verifier can look up in a registry. Verifiers check the token and then resolve the issuer to a registered organization before trusting the agent. *When:* Use when agents act across organizational boundaries and need a verifiable, expiring identity and an audit path back to a responsible party. The talk does not specify the registry or blockchain mechanics, so treat this as the concept only.
- **Decision markets for policy** — Any member proposes a policy. Members buy pass or fail tokens instead of casting votes. A member's token value rises when it sides with the group that wins, so the market price reflects the aggregate belief about the outcome. *When:* Use as a thought experiment for multi-agent decision-making, where agents are goal-directed and the designer wants arguments to be tested rather than just persuasive. The talk gives no evidence, so the claim of better decisions is unverified.
- **Per-action audit trail back to a responsible principal** — Record each agent action with the originating ally and organization, and link it to a registered identity. The talk suggests anchoring this on a blockchain so that a dispute can trace a chain of actions to the party responsible. *When:* Use when liability or accountability for autonomous agent actions matters. Note that the talk addresses who is responsible after an action, not how to block the injected instruction that caused it.

## Hot takes
- The talk is a product pitch for Kaduna Club, ending with an early-access sign-up. Sponsored or self-promotional content with thin substance caps quality at 2.
- The claim that decision markets produce better decisions cites unnamed 'science' and gives no data.
- The claim that blockchains are the right solution for agent identity is asserted, not shown. The line 'you can't trick the blockchain' is unsupported, since the trust still depends on the registry and the keys.
- The talk states the trifecta clearly but offers no defense against prompt injection of untrusted content itself. Its proposed answer is identity and audit after the fact, which does not stop an agent from being hijacked.
- The legal claims (securities risk on profit distribution, DUNA standing, the designer being a16z) are stated without sources and should not be relied on without legal review.
- The historical analogy (LambdaMOO culture, extractive platforms) is a personal narrative rather than evidence.
